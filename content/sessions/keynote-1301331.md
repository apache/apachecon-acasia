Embodied AI is driving the expansion of artificial intelligence from the digital world into the physical world. Yet its development remains constrained by several factors: relatively closed software stacks built around a limited number of computing platforms, costly data that is difficult to reuse, and fragmented toolchains across simulation, training, evaluation, and deployment.

This talk explores how to build an open, portable, and commercially sustainable embodied AI ecosystem—around infrastructure, data, and models. In light of the impact that generative AI has had on software production methods and traditional open-source business models, the talk will also reexamine the boundaries of open source: which infrastructure, interfaces, standards, and evaluation capabilities are suitable for open co-development, and which data, models, and industry-specific capabilities should retain commercial differentiation. Open source needs to strike a new balance among technological autonomy, ecosystem collaboration, commercial returns, and long-term maintainability. The large-scale development of embodied AI should not be built upon dependence on a single model, a single simulator, or a single compute platform—it requires an open ecosystem that connects infrastructure, data, models, compute vendors, robotics companies, research institutions, and developers.

Jiaqi Sun: Motphys, Co-founder (谋先飞联合创始人)

Jiaqi Sun, co-founder of Motphys, PhD from the Department of Mechanics at Peking University. He has extensive research and industry experience in physics simulation, computational geometry and graphics algorithms, heterogeneous CPU/GPU computing, and the integration of artificial intelligence with scientific computing. For more than a decade, he has worked at the forefront of industrial physics solver development, leading the creation of integrated software that connects geometric processing with multiphysics simulation. The resulting technologies and products have been applied across automotive engineering, aerospace, data centers, renewable energy, semiconductor manufacturing, and other industrial simulation domains.
In recent years, Jiaqi has focused on integrating physics simulation with embodied AI at Motphys. He has driven the development of the Motrix family of simulation and training platforms and the MotrixBench open simulation benchmarking framework, while also supporting academic collaborations such as GS-Playground and UniLab. His work explores open infrastructure for Real2Sim2Real pipelines, robot learning, and heterogeneous computing platforms. He also studies how to build an open-source embodied AI ecosystem that balances technological independence, collaborative innovation, and long-term commercial sustainability.
